Triadic Orientation Assessment
VERTICAL· HORIZONTAL· TEMPORAL

This instrument maps where you stand inside a three-axis model of conscious experience. It does not assign a personality type. It locates an orientation — a position inside a geometric interior — and describes the shape of that interior.

Every person is oriented along three axes simultaneously. Where you sit determines the zone you occupy; the rest of the report describes the texture of that position: subtype, shape, capture, signature, deformation.

Theoretical Basis

Based on the Triaxial Orientation Theory framework (P.R. Erickson, 2025). Three orthogonal axes generate eight zones (octants of an octahedron), 24 subtypes (3 per zone), and a continuous shape parameter (Lp norm) that describes how costly it is to hold multiple orientations at once.

Condensation Methodology

This form is a 36-item condensation of the original 66-item v2.0 instrument. Selection was driven by the v1.0→v2.0 validity audit, which classified items as CLEAN, GRAY (cross-axis or ambiguous), or PROBLEM (measuring the wrong construct).

For each pole the 5 cleanest items were retained based on: (1) experiential rather than aspirational language; (2) forced tradeoff or specific behavior rather than generic agreement; (3) low cross-axis loading; (4) low social desirability variance. The 6 reverse-coded integration items were kept; the 6 positive-coded ones were dropped — the audit identified those as adding noise (self-reported capacity is overestimated, while felt cost is reported honestly).

What Is Computed

Pole scores (six 0–1 means) → axis scores (signed differences + temporal extension) → zone (octant classification) → subtype (nearest of three interior biases) → shape parameter (blended self-report + empirical entropy-based estimate) → asymmetric geometry (per-axis health, scale, exchange rates between axis pairs) → capture analysis (vertex/edge/face proximity) → stamp detection (correlation against five inherited deformation profiles).

Pre-validation research instrument. Not a diagnostic tool. Results describe a current orientation, not a fixed identity.
